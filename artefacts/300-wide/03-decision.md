# 03-decision

**Owner:** Sarah Chen — Head of CX

## Chosen change
Show **estimated availability with a visible confidence / uncertainty cue** on the product page instead of a binary “In stock” promise.

## Rationale
This option won because it directly addresses the core journey break: the customer is harmed by false certainty before committing to pickup. It scored highest on impact while also being the easiest meaningful change to deliver, which makes it the strongest first intervention.

## Runner-up
**“Reserve & we’ll confirm in 10 min” hold**

## Why the chosen change beat the runner-up
The runner-up offers stronger reassurance, but it adds more operational and technical effort because it changes reservation logic and inventory handling. The chosen change is a better first move because it reduces false promises earlier in the journey, is faster to test, and can later be combined with stronger confirmation patterns if needed.

## Adversarial challenge
The main challenge to the top pick is that weak wording could still confuse the user or reduce trust if the confidence cue is not clear enough. The decision stands, but the wording and freshness signal must be validated in the prototype and test plan.
