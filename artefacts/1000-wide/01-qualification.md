# Meridian Retail Group — qualification memo

## Fit (1–5) with rationale
**Capability fit: 5** — The ask aligns directly with the carried-forward chain we already built: checkout service, AI gateway integration, security pack, ops pack, and delivery framing.

**Delivery fit: 4** — The work is deliverable, but the timeline is only realistic if scope control, named client-side owners, and rollback / support decisions are locked early.

**Commercial fit: 3** — The opportunity is credible, but the AI-metered operating cost and the likely need for fixed-price pressure create margin risk unless assumptions are bounded tightly.

**Strategic fit: 5** — This is a high-value reference shape: customer-facing commerce, AI governance, security, and operations in one deal.

## Win themes (3)
1. **Integrated end-to-end delivery** — We can show one coherent chain from opportunity framing through architecture, engineering, QA, data, operations, and security rather than a disconnected proposal.
2. **Governed AI delivery** — We can differentiate on AI control, gateway usage, cost caps, risk evidence, and security posture instead of pitching AI as an unbounded feature.
3. **Operations-and-support readiness from day one** — The buyer is not only buying build capacity; they are buying deploy, rollback, incident, support, and cost-control discipline.

## Deal-breakers (at least 1)
If Meridian insists on **fixed-price without scope assumptions, capped client-side dependencies, and explicit AI spend governance**, this should become **no-bid**.

## Top 3 risks
| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed-price compression against evolving checkout + AI scope | High | High | Bid with conditions: explicit assumptions, change-control, open-items log, and excluded scope beyond the defined checkout chain |
| AI operating cost or governance concern weakens buyer confidence | Medium | High | Put AI cost cap, gateway control, kill-switch, and evidence pack in the proposal front section rather than as appendices |
| Delivery timeline slips due to client-side dependencies and decision latency | Medium | High | Name required Meridian-side owners, decision rights, and dependency dates in the rollout plan and make them contractual assumptions |

## Recommendation
**Bid with conditions.** This is a strong strategic and capability fit, and we have a credible differentiator if we lead with governed AI delivery and operational readiness rather than generic implementation language. The pursuit should proceed only if the commercial model, AI spend controls, and client dependency assumptions are made explicit early; otherwise margin and delivery risk are too high for a clean bid.
